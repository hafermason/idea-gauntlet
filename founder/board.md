# Board: 3D Walkthroughs (working name)

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 pass.** Average score **5.3 / 10** (Offers 6, Monopoly 4, Product 6).

## Risks raised by more than one member (most important first)

1. **Market size is unknown.** Offers and Monopoly both ask how many Utah luxury builders fit the target and how many homes each starts a year. One-off, per-home revenue sold one lunch at a time may cap this at side income. *Answered by: founder-competitors / founder-cfo + real counts.*
2. **No moat, and copying is easy.** All three point out that AI tools plus Blender are open to anyone, including the builder's own architect or designer. Offers calls it commodity pressure. Monopoly gives it no secret and no 10x advantage. Product says the edge has to come from craft and quality. *Answered by: founder-competitors.*
3. **It is a deliverable, not an outcome.** Offers wants it sold as "your client signs sooner / haggles less". Product wants the homebuyer's first five minutes defined. Both say the walkthrough file alone is a nice extra, and nice extras get cut. *Answered by: founder-offer.*
4. **Hours, not dollars, are the limit.** All three note that the ~$10 direct cost makes margin a non-issue. The real limits are the founder's hours (including revisions) and the lunches it takes to land each builder. *Answered by: founder-cfo / founder-ops.*

## Conditions checklist

- [ ] Paid proof: at least 3 builders pay list price after seeing a free sample, within 90 days (Offers). Builders keep paying, with the cost of landing each builder recorded (Monopoly).
- [ ] Size the market: number of target Utah builders × homes per year, enough to reach the income goal (Offers, Monopoly).
- [ ] Name the competitors builders can reach today and their prices, plus one clear way this is 10x better on something builders care about (Monopoly).
- [ ] Rebuild the offer as a named sales package around the builder's goal: turnaround guarantee, per-builder recurring terms (Offers).
- [ ] Name the moat to build: exclusive builder relationships, a reusable asset/finish library, or being built into how the builder prices and sells (Monopoly).
- [ ] One page on the homebuyer's first five minutes with the walkthrough: device, setting, guided or free (Product).
- [ ] A written quality bar and a check against the plan; sign-off from 2 builders on real samples (Product).
- [ ] The delivery loop defined: format, branding, revisions included, turnaround. Track real hours per home including revisions (Product).
- [ ] Tiers explained in one line each, or merged (Product).

## Where the members disagree

Monopoly scores it lowest (4). It sees a service business with no secret that will be undercut. Offers and Product (6 each) think a reachable, wealthy niche plus a strong free-sample sales motion is enough to start, if the offer and quality are sharpened. Offers sees the low cost as room to stack bonuses. Product sees "a few hours per home" as a warning about quality at the luxury end.

## The strongest version the board can see

Not "a 3D walkthrough per home" but **the pre-construction sales package for Utah luxury builders.** Each builder signs a standing agreement: every new plan gets a branded, plan-accurate interactive walkthrough, a flythrough video and listing-ready images, with a set turnaround. Upgrade and finish variants let the homebuyer see what they're paying for. Every builder adds to a reusable finish library, so each home is faster to make and harder to copy. Sold as "your clients commit sooner and pick more upgrades", and priced per builder rather than per lunch. That differs from the pitch in two ways: recurring builder accounts instead of one-off homes, and an outcome instead of a file.
