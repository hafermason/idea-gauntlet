# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- It fits in one sentence: "We turn your floor plans into a 3D walkthrough your client can see before you break ground." That clarity is rare and it matters.
- The sales motion is the product. A free walkthrough of the builder's own plan, shown in person, lets the builder experience the result before any pitch. Starting from the experience is exactly what the Product lens asks for.
- The scope is narrow: one kind of buyer (luxury Utah builders), one input (2D plans), one output. Little needs to be cut.
- On the stated numbers (a few hours and about $10 per home), it can afford to spend extra time on craft where it counts.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The quality doesn't match the price of the home.** Luxury buyers notice details. If an AI-plus-Blender model looks generic or slightly wrong (proportions, light, materials, a window in the wrong place), it makes the builder look worse, not better. And if it shows finishes that will not actually be built, it sets expectations the builder then has to manage. "A few hours" per home is both a strength and a warning sign.
2. **The end experience isn't defined.** The person who finally uses this is the builder's client, not the builder. What do they actually do: click a link on a phone, explore freely, watch a guided video, sit with the builder on a screen? The basic and premium tiers blur this, since "walkthrough" vs. "flythrough" isn't explained. Until the first five minutes for the homebuyer are described, the product isn't finished.
3. **Seams after the sale.** Plans change, finishes get chosen late, and clients ask for "what if" versions. Every revision, handoff of files, and delivery format is a place where things can break or where those few hours turn into many. None of this is covered yet.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Describe the moment the homebuyer first opens it. What device, where, who is with them, and what do they feel?
- What is the one thing it must do brilliantly: accuracy to the plan, emotional "this is my home," or speed? Which would you give up?
- What exactly separates the $1,500 tier from the $4,000 tier, and could one tier be cut?
- How accurate is a few hours of work compared with the real plan? Who checks it, and what happens when it's wrong?
- How are revisions handled, and how many are included?
- How does the builder receive and share it, and does it carry the builder's brand or yours?
- Open numbers: revision rate, real hours per home including fixes, turnaround time, and how many sample walkthroughs have been shown so far and with what reaction.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Write a one-page description of the homebuyer's first five minutes with the walkthrough, and build the product to deliver exactly that.
2. Set and enforce a written quality bar (accuracy to the plan, lighting, materials) plus a check against the plan before anything is shown. Get sign-off from at least two builders on real samples.
3. Define the full delivery loop: the handoff format, builder branding, the number of revisions included, and turnaround. Track the real hours per home, including revisions, on the first paid jobs.
4. Make the difference between tiers clear in one line each, or merge them into one.
